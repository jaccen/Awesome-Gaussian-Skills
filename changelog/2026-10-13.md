## Part 1 — cg-paper-writing v3.2.0 → v3.3.0

## Summary

Codified the **comparison dimension consistency rule (对比同维度铁律)** into the cg-paper-writing
skill: in comparison experiments, every compared method (including baselines) must be evaluated
on the same dataset — same version, resolution, and split — under the same evaluation protocol;
numbers whose caliber cannot be verified must be rerun under unified settings; numbers from
different dimensions must never share one comparison table. The rule is now enforced at three
layers: experiment-section writing templates, the claim verification workflow, and Gate 1 / Gate 2
integrity checks.

## New Rule: Comparison Fairness Protocol — 同维度对比铁律

Six clauses, enforced wherever comparison tables are written or audited:

1. **Same dataset** — identical dataset version, scene/image list, and train/test split for every
   compared method. Resolution counts as part of the dataset: full-resolution and downsampled
   evaluations are different dimensions and must never share a column.
2. **Same evaluation protocol** — identical metric definitions and implementations (PSNR mask
   convention; SSIM/LPIPS library and backbone — VGG vs Alex are NOT interchangeable; image
   loading / tonemapping pipeline).
3. **Same training conditions, or full per-cell disclosure** — iteration count, schedule, and
   hardware aligned to the baseline when feasible; otherwise marked (e.g., †) and explained in the
   caption.
4. **Number provenance priority** — (a) best: rerun the baseline in our own environment (retrain or
   re-evaluate the official checkpoint under our protocol); (b) acceptable: cite original-paper
   numbers only after an item-by-item caliber check, with the source marked; (c) forbidden: a
   number whose caliber does not match ours — rerun the experiment instead of copying.
5. **Rerun for verification** — rerunning under unified settings is the default fix for
   untraceable baseline numbers and the strongest rebuttal response to "unfair comparison"
   challenges.
6. **One table, one dimension** — never mix resolutions, dataset versions, protocols, or
   "cited-from-paper" vs "reproduced-by-us" numbers in one column without per-cell markers and a
   caption note; split into separate tables when dimensions differ.
