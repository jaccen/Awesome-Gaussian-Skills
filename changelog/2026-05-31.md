## Notable Trends

1. **Probability-based densification eliminates ADC**: EulerianGS (CVPR 2026) replaces the entire heuristic clone/split/prune pipeline with gradient-based probability density optimization via hashed probability pyramids. This is the most fundamental challenge to 3DGS's densification mechanism since the original paper. If validated, ADC could become a legacy component.

2. **Uncertainty-aware 3DGS emerges**: GAVIS introduces the first principled uncertainty quantification for 3DGS at 200 FPS via anisotropic visibility fields. This enables active mapping (next-best-view) — previously impossible without re-training or heuristic proxies.

3. **Physical simulation + 3DGS integration deepens**: Three new papers (PhyGenHOI, MonoPhysics, FreeForm) integrate differentiable physics simulation (MPM, RKPM) with 3DGS, enabling joint geometry/appearance/physics optimization from monocular or multi-view input.

4. **Autonomous driving 3DGS diversifies**: FRUC (cooperative driving), DeGO (deformable occupancy), WeatherCity (weather editing) expand the AD 3DGS ecosystem across calibration-free reconstruction, dynamic occupancy, and controllable environment simulation.

5. **Mobile 3DGS training**: PocketGS demonstrates on-device 3DGS training, closing the capture-to-rendering loop on mobile hardware — a significant step toward consumer 3D scanning applications.

6. **Scene Graph + 3DGS**: DGSG-Mind is the first to combine dynamic 3D Gaussian scene graphs with embodied reasoning agents, bridging 3DGS reconstruction with high-level scene understanding and robot task planning.

### Spatial Intelligence Trend Notes

7. **Perception-Action Loop Closure**: ESI-Bench represents a paradigm shift from passive observation-based spatial QA to active embodied perception-action evaluation. Current top models (GPT-4o, Gemini) fail at spatial interaction despite strong visual recognition. This directly impacts 3DGS-based embodied reasoning methods (GSMem, RoboSplat).

8. **Hierarchical Spatial Cognition**: HiSpatial (CVPR 2026) and SpatialStack demonstrate that 3D spatial understanding in VLMs benefits from hierarchical decomposition — from low-level 3D structure inference to high-level spatial relation reasoning — rather than treating spatial understanding as a monolithic capability.
